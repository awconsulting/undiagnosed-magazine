A Day in the Gray Zone

7:12 a.m.

The first thought is not good morning. It’s a scan. How much pain? How much fog? How many spoons? The spoon thing comes from Christine Miserandino, who explained life with lupus by handing a friend a fistful of spoons, each one a unit of energy. You start with a limited number. Everything costs one or more. Showering. A phone call. A doctor’s appointment. The scan is logistics.

9:40 a.m.

You open the patient portal to check whether the referral went through. It didn’t. You make a note to call. You will make the call later, when you have the energy to be pleasant and persistent at the same time.

12:05 p.m.

A friend texts: How are you feeling? You type better, thanks! and delete it. You type still waiting for answers and delete it. You type honestly, not great and stare at it. You send a heart emoji.
