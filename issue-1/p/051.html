Social media

And then I made a flare book.

Somewhere around TikTok number 3,000, I started screenshotting the comments instead of the videos. The ones so unhinged I felt like I was reading a language only I understood.

A woman asking if she could use her cat’s diabetes medication. A man explaining that he and his wife communicate only through a shared Notes app. Someone’s grandmother whose whole comment was “I hope you step on a Lego.”

I collected them. Thousands of them. Then I put them in a paperback. It’s the internet at its most unhinged, formatted so I can open it when I can’t look at a screen without my brain hurting.

It’s the book I keep next to my bed. The one I reach for when I’m too tired to watch anything and too wired to sleep. The one that reminds me people are weird and funny and alive.
