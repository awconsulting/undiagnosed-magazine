The Grief That Has No Funeral

I have lost four years. I have lost the version of me who could say yes to dinner without calculating the cost. I have lost the woman who didn’t have to rehearse a doctor’s appointment like a court appearance. I have lost the assumption that my body would do what I asked it to.

There is no ceremony for this. No casserole. No card. No “sorry for your loss” because technically nothing died. Except the life I had planned.

Researchers call this ambiguous loss. Grief without closure, without a clear event, without a socially sanctioned moment of recognition. In studies of parents facing undiagnosed conditions, the same feelings keep coming up. Uncertainty, guilt, isolation, hopelessness, shock.

The grief doesn’t follow a tidy arc.
